Tethered

An immersive dance-theatre experience about losing touch with yourself, where a haptic wearable syncs each audience member to the performers' inner lives.

Outcome

Tethered debuted at 93 Feet Live in London and ran at the Edinburgh Fringe in 2025, where it was reviewed by Edinburgh Festivals Magazine. The work is in ongoing development as a full-scale immersive experience.

Synopsis

When you arrive, someone fits you with a device around your torso. You're told it's for your health: government mandated, nothing to worry about.

Around you, the world is in crisis. Set in a near future where wearable technology monitors and regulates the body, the device pulses in response to what's happening on stage, syncing you to two characters living with early-onset dementia.

Objects pass between performers and audience, each one carrying someone's memory. But not everyone's device is the same, and not everything you've been told about it is true.

My approach

I led the experiential and creative R&D direction, defining how performers and audiences interact within a multi-sensory performance environment. Working from an existing script, I translated its themes into embodied audience experience rather than rewriting story or dialogue.

I gave the audience an active role inside the world of the piece, and designed the interaction framework through which they inhabit it: how gaze, proximity and position create moments of intimacy, tension and recognition. Objects became memory carriers that audiences hold, witness and pass on. Alongside this, I developed a proof of concept integrating haptic wearables into the narrative, deepening embodiment without overriding the live performance.
