Outcome

The first iteration, which I began developing in 2022, secured follow-on funding, scaling into a larger multidisciplinary team and expanding beyond the initial R&D phase. In 2025 it was showcased at the National Digital Twin Centre launch to policymakers, industry leaders, academics and press.

The brief

Industrial digital twins are usually built for engineers: dense, data-led and screen-bound. The question was how to turn a working industrial simulation into something legible, compelling and spatially present for non-technical stakeholders, decision-makers and national policymakers.

The experience

A visitor puts on a headset, the room disappears, and they are standing inside a running factory rather than watching it on a screen. Pressing buttons in VR starts and stops the real conveyor belt in the room.

The design

I led concept development and early-stage design, layering immersive and multi-sensory systems onto the existing twin so people could step inside it. That meant working across three registers at once: the technical architecture of the simulation, the spatial and sensory logic of the experience, and interaction design that lets a non-specialist understand a complex system in real time.

I authored the system architecture and the full technical documentation, covering VR integration, haptic glove setup, troubleshooting and a user manual.

What's next

For the enhanced version, I proposed treating geography as a design material: capturing Digital Catapult's London, Belfast and Newcastle offices with Gaussian splatting and placing them in one shared world, so someone in Belfast could reach into the London space and operate the physical machine hundreds of miles away.
