Living Archives

An archival Off-White dress, brought back to life in mixed reality. Wearing headsets, the audience watches a photorealistic performer appear in the room in front of them, wearing the garment.

Outcome

Living Archives premiered at a private showcase in London on 22 October 2026, presented to an invited audience from fashion, culture and technology.

The brief

Digital Catapult joined the University of Manchester's UKRI-funded research as technology and delivery partner, to prove one idea: that an archival garment could be brought back to life as an immersive, cyber-physical experience.

The garment was an Off-White Pre-Fall 2020 silk maxi dress. I designed the experience end to end, from the creative concept to the technical architecture and production pipeline.

The concept

The experience takes place in a Frankenstein-style laboratory. It nods to the Romantic references in the dress's print, and to Virgil Abloh's practice of claiming authorship by altering something that already exists. Here the specimen jars hold the garment's parts — print, construction, brand — rather than body parts.

The performer is newly "born", discovering her body inside the dress at the same moment the audience does. Post-production borrows from Abloh's DJ background: clips freeze, stutter and layer rather than cut cleanly, so she feels almost human, but not quite.

The experience

A projected film first walks visitors through the laboratory and out across a Romantic landscape. Then, as they put on headsets alongside one another, a photorealistic woman appears in the physical room. She moves through the space theatrically, and as she comes closer, the finer details of the dress come into view.

The build

I designed a split architecture: the Gaussian splat performer runs inside the headset, while the environment plays on external projection — a parallel to virtual production. Unity acts as the master clock on a looping timeline and drives Unreal Engine over OSC, so visitors can join the experience at any point.
